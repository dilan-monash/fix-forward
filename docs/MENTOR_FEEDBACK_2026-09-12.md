## Two family situations to test

These are **design hypotheses**, not proven demographic segments:

1. **“We need a practical appliance decision now.”** An adult needs help with warning signs, repair contacts, a price comparison or a recycling service. Success means reaching an understandable next action without unnecessary reading or compulsory model knowledge.
2. **“We want to learn together.”** An adult and child have time for fictional stories about noticing concerns, asking about repair and responsible disposal. Success means the child stays interested, can explain a discovery and understands that a grown-up handles real appliance problems.

The same family can move between these situations. The optional learning activity does not establish that the whole practical decision flow is suitable for children to use independently.

## Proposed recognition workflow — not implemented

1. The adult deliberately chooses a label/photo or barcode option; the manual path stays available.
2. The system suggests only supported identity details, such as appliance category, brand or model. A barcode match requires a verified identifier-to-model record. Missing, uncertain, ambiguous and conflicting matches remain explicit.
3. The adult confirms or corrects those details. The app does not silently choose a model or substitute another brand.
4. A short report shows **“What we could identify”** and **“What we couldn't determine”**, including the fact that the input cannot establish an internal fault, clear a recall or confirm that a facility accepts the item.
5. The confirmed identity feeds the existing product-specific safety checklist and recall logic, then the existing results and service options.

Camera denial, unsupported hardware/browser behaviour, unreadable labels, poor images, absent catalogue entries and network failures must lead back to useful manual entry. Upload/storage decisions and evaluation evidence would need to be documented as part of a future implementation. The present backend camera restriction has not been relaxed.
