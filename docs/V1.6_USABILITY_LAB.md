# FixForward v1.6 — Usability Lab Candidate

## Why this version exists

v1.3 fixed major reliability and safety-integrity defects. v1.4 changed the journey to start from user goals. v1.5 simplified the language, map/service cards and product-specific safety questions. v1.6 is a further usability-lab candidate focused on the friction identified while walking through the product as an ordinary household user.

The central product rule is:

> **Useful action first. Evidence and technical detail second. Safety still overrides convenience when it must.**

## User problem

A household user does not usually arrive thinking:

> “I want to run a product-recall triage and inspect repair-evidence provenance.”

They are more likely to think:

- “Can I repair this?”
- “Is repair worth the money?”
- “Where can I take it?”
- “How do I recycle it?”
- “I don't know what I should do.”

The interface therefore begins with those intents.

## v1.6 changes

### 1. Safety uncertainty no longer ends the questionnaire

In v1.5, Not sure could act like an early stop. That created a poor interaction: the user might be unsure about one question but know the answers to the others.

v1.6 behavior:

- Not sure is recorded as uncertainty.
- The user completes the remaining short, relevant questions.
- The final result is uncertain unless a critical Yes is also present.
- A critical Yes takes priority over uncertainty.
- **I'm not able to check this safely** remains a separate explicit way to stop.

### 2. Mixed safety answers are meaningful

v1.6 avoids “every Yes means the exact same error.”

- smoke/fire/burning, shock/sparks/exposed wire, badly damaged plug/cord, water ingress and damaged battery are critical;
- unusual heat and repeated power trips/new popping/buzzing are caution-level;
- Not sure is uncertain;
- any critical Yes wins even if several answers are No.

The user-facing messages are different for serious, caution and uncertain states.

### 3. Serious warning has a useful disposal route

A serious safety result still blocks community Repair Café and ordinary cost comparison, but it no longer leaves someone who wants disposal with no next step.

The user can open controlled recycling/disposal planning. The screen warns:

> Do not transport an appliance that is hot, smoking, leaking or actively damaged.

The facility list remains contact/check-first information and never claims the item is safe to move or accepted.

### 4. Repair user gets repair + cost choices

After a clear Repair check, the user sees:

- **Find repair options**
- **Estimate & compare costs**

They do not have to return home or repeat the whole journey to compare money after looking at repair.

### 5. Location autocomplete

The manual location input now behaves like a familiar location search:

- `312` can show matching 312x postcode/suburb pairs;
- suburb prefixes such as `rich` can show Richmond;
- the dropdown supports mouse, touch and keyboard selection;
- final search input is validated before results are shown.

The suggestions come from the project's local Melbourne-area suburb index, so every keystroke is not sent to an external geocoding service.

### 6. Map fallback is bounded

The service list is the core functionality; the map is supporting UI. If the Leaflet dependency hangs, the app waits a bounded eight seconds and falls back instead of displaying “Loading map…” forever.

### 7. Cost helper becomes more realistic

The interaction borrows the useful idea from fast repair-or-replace tools: identify the appliance, describe the problem in normal words, and get useful context quickly.

But v1.6 refuses to imitate capabilities it does not actually have:

- no photo/OCR in I1;
- no external AI call in this prototype;
- no proprietary model database;
- no claim that a typed model has been verified for pricing;
- no fake replacement price.

The screen shows source-linked published repair-service pricing examples separately and labels them as context, not the user's quote. Users with real prices can still make a direct arithmetic comparison.

### 8. Input validation is treated as UX and security

Limits now cover:

- brand;
- model;
- problem description;
- suburb/postcode;
- repair quote;
- replacement price.

Errors are field-specific and focus moves to the first invalid field. Limits reduce accidental rubbish input, overly long text and misleading calculations without rejecting legitimate model numbers.

## Evidence hierarchy

The interface follows:

```text
WHAT CAN I DO NOW?
      ↓
WHERE / HOW MUCH?
      ↓
WHAT DOES THE EVIDENCE SAY?
      ↓
HOW WAS THE EVIDENCE COLLECTED?
```

That is why the Repair page puts service/location actions before the Open Repair Alliance chart and puts provenance in expandable detail.

## What v1.6 is not

It is not:

- a complete national recall checker;
- a diagnosis tool;
- a guarantee that an appliance is safe;
- a directory of verified licensed repair technicians;
- a live retailer price-search engine;
- an Appliance911.ai clone;
- a completed AI price estimator;
- final approved Iteration-1 scope.
